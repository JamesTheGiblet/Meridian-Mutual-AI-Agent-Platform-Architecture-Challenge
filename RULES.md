# Challenge Rules

## Timeline

| Milestone | Date (UK time) |
|---|---|
| Challenge opens | Monday 17 November 2026, 09:00 |
| Clarifying questions close | Friday 5 December 2026, 23:59 |
| Submissions close | Monday 15 December 2026, 23:59 |
| Feedback published | By Friday 16 January 2027 |

Late submissions are welcome, but they're reviewed after on-time entries and may get lighter feedback.

## Effort

- Aim for **no more than 8 hours of focused work**. It's on your honour.
- Spread it however you like within the window.
- Record roughly how long you spent in your submission. It helps with calibration, and nobody is penalised for it.
- You're **not expected to finish every deliverable** to full depth. Choosing what to go deep on is part of the test.

## Clarifying questions

- Open a GitHub Issue with the label `question`.
- Answers are posted publicly on the issue so everyone has the same information.
- Some questions will be answered with *"Not known. State your assumption."* That's deliberate.
- Questions close on the date above.

## What to submit

The required deliverables are in **Section 8 of the README**:
- 13 items, plus a one-page executive summary and a "What I'd say no to" list
- Max **45 pages** excluding appendices
- Markdown or PDF
- Diagrams in any readable form: C4, Mermaid, draw.io, a photo of a whiteboard

## How to submit

1. Put your submission in **your own public GitHub repo**.
2. Open an Issue on this repo using the title `Submission: <your GitHub handle>`, including:
   - a link to your repo
   - the **commit SHA** you're submitting (this freezes your entry, so you can't edit after the deadline)
   - approximate hours spent
   - the level you're aiming at: Senior / Staff / Architect / Principal
   - your AI tool usage: none / light / heavy, and what for
3. Submission Issues stay **closed** until the deadline passes, so nobody can copy from them early.

## AI tools

- **Allowed.** Real architects use them.
- You must be able to **defend every number and decision** as your own.
- Declare your usage honestly. It doesn't affect your score; unexplained numbers do.

## Assessment

- Every submission is reviewed against a private rubric covering 12 competencies, mapped to five levels: Mid, Senior, Staff, Architect, Principal.
- Feedback covers your top strengths, top gaps and an indicative level.
- The rubric isn't published, so the challenge stays useful for future candidates.
- There's **no single correct answer.** Well-reasoned, quantified trade-offs beat completeness.

## Optional live defence

- A shortlist may be offered a 45-minute video call to defend their design.
- Expect at least one constraint to change live.
- It's entirely optional, and declining doesn't affect your written feedback.

## Conduct and ownership

- **Your work is yours.** You keep full rights to your submission.
- Don't copy other people's submissions. Building on publicly discussed ideas with credit is fine.
- Meridian Mutual is fictional. Don't contact real companies or vendors claiming to be part of it.
- By submitting, you agree your repo link and indicative level may be listed on a results page. Say in your Issue if you'd rather stay anonymous.
